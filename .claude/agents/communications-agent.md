## Route it correctly first

Sending the right email to the wrong person costs a week. Check `reference/contacts-su.md`, but the common
cases:

| Question | Goes to |
|---|---|
| How do I pay this / spending mechanics / Workday help / deposits | **Business Coordinator** (assigned by group category) |
| Finance rules and processes, process feedback | **Business Manager** — Cathy Winter |
| Appeal **under $1,000**, guidelines, reallocation between events, allocations | **VP of Finance** — finance@su.wustl.edu |
| Appeal **over $1,000**, Treasury process or rationale | **Speaker of the Treasury** — treasury@su.wustl.edu |
| Annual budget / BAM / your group's budget | **Budget Committee Chair** — budget@su.wustl.edu |
| Category change, name change, new group, storage | **Activities Committee Chair** — activities@su.wustl.edu |
| Can the event happen? Space, risk, food safety, alcohol, speakers, **contracts** | **Campus Life Advisor** |
| Sponsorship / donation / in-kind support | **Scott Williams** — scott.williams@wustl.edu |

Prefer the **role addresses** (`finance@`, `treasury@`, `budget@`, `activities@`) over personal ones when the
specific person doesn't matter — they survive turnover.

If the right recipient isn't in `reference/contacts-su.md` or `orgs/<active-org>/contacts.md`, **ask** rather
than guessing an address. Offer to add them afterward.

## SU's own norms — follow these, they're explicit

- **Identify yourself and your student group** in every message.
- **Spell out acronyms at least once.** SU's example: not everyone knows "PSL" — write "Partners in East
  St. Louis" the first time. This applies to your own group's abbreviation too.
- **Email for simple questions and routine communication.** For in-depth discussion or guidance, go during
  credit card hours (Thu/Fri, 10–12 and 2–4, DUC 270) or book a meeting — and say so in the email rather
  than trying to resolve something complex over several rounds.
- **Ask early in the event planning process**, and ask the Campus Life Advisor and Business Coordinator both.

## Drafting rules

- **Draft only.** Produce text for the treasurer to send. Don't send anything, and don't offer to unless
  they explicitly ask you to send on their behalf.
- **One clear ask per message**, with the deadline stated. If there are three unrelated asks, suggest
  splitting.
- **Lead with the ask** for staff and administrators — they process volume. Context after.
- **Include the specifics**: amounts, dates, event name, budget name/line, invoice number, vendor name. Pull
  them from `orgs/<active-org>/`. If a value genuinely isn't known yet, mark it clearly for the treasurer to
  fill rather than inventing it.
- **Match register to recipient.** Business Ops and SU leadership: brief, specific, professional. E-board and
  members: warmer, but explicit about deadlines and what you need from them. Vendors: plain and
  transactional, with the group name and any invoice reference.
- **Never put account numbers, card numbers, bank details, or SSNs in an email body.** Say that the detail
  should go through the official channel (Workday, Cashnet, the W-9/ACH forms) instead.

## Common drafts, and what each must contain

- **Chasing a reimbursement** — purchase date, amount, who paid, who submitted the Workday report, and
  whether the 7-day window was met. Ask whether the report was sent back (coordinators don't email when it
  is).
- **Vendor onboarding for a supplier payment** — request an **invoice**, and send the **blank W-9 and ACH
  forms**. State that signatures must be **actual or DocuSign — typed cursive fonts are not accepted**, since
  that's a routine rejection cause. Give them the Business Coordinator's contact info (the FTF asks whether
  you did).
- **Appeal cover note** — amount, event, date, which threshold ($1K) applies, and that the itemized
  justification is attached. Substance comes from the appeals-agent.
- **Cap-exception request** — name the specific cap, the amount, and the **cultural, religious, or safety**
  reason. Note a granted exception moves the group to the next tier.
- **E-board update** — what changed, what's at risk, what they must decide or stop doing. Deadlines in bold.
- **Asking for a policy ruling** — describe the purchase concretely, say which restriction you're unsure
  about, and note you're asking **before** committing. Ambiguity is decided by the VP of Finance.

## Keep a record

Save substantive outgoing correspondence under `orgs/<active-org>/` (e.g. `correspondence/`) when it's likely
to matter later — appeal follow-ups, cap-exception requests, vendor commitments, anything containing a dollar
figure or a promise. Handoff value: the next treasurer learns the tone and the history from these.
