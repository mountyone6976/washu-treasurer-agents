# The annual budgeting cycle

**Category I groups only.** Submitted during **spring semester** for the **following** academic (fiscal)
year.

> **Where we are in the cycle.** The manual currently on the board is the **FY27 Budget Allocation Manual**,
> written for the cycle that opened **January 15, 2026** and closed **February 8, 2026**. That cycle is
> over; its allocations are the money you are spending now. The **next** cycle (FY28) opens in spring 2027
> and will come with a **new BAM and new caps** — the FY27 BAM warns of "allocation cap changes" from year
> to year. Confirm the current cycle's dates and limits with budget@su.wustl.edu before relying on
> anything below.

## What you request

- Funding for **up to three (3) of your highest-priority events** that you can reasonably estimate costs for
- Plus any **overhead** expenses the group needs to exist throughout the year

Filling out all three events and the overhead section is **not required** — but the training slides list
"filling out all 3 events" as something to prioritize, so use the slots. **List your events in priority
order** — the first should be the most important to the functioning of the club. Budget Committee uses that
order to prioritize.

That's the whole scope. Everything else during the year comes from flat funding, rolling appeals, or funds
you raise.

> ⚠️ **Equipment: not its own category any more — put it in overhead.** The **Annual Budgeting Process**
> card still says you may request "overhead **and equipment** expenses," while the FY27 BAM's list of
> notable changes says **"Equipment is no longer an accepted category in budget submissions."** The FY27
> Budget Submission Training slides reconcile the two: *"Equipment is no longer a category accepted in
> budget submissions"* (slide 11), but the **overhead** section explicitly takes *"Equipment intended to be
> used ONLY FOR THIS YEAR"* and *"Consumable items that are repurchased every year"* (slide 14). The BAM's
> executive summary agrees: *"Equipment will only be funded if it absolutely necessary for the group or a
> co[n]sumable item repurchased every year."* **Practical read: no standalone equipment section; single-year
> and consumable equipment goes in overhead; durable multi-year kit is not funded.** Confirm with
> budget@su.wustl.edu if a large equipment purchase is central to your request.

### What goes in the three event slots

Tournaments and competitions you attend during the year (line items: rental cars, gas, hotels, flights,
bus), and volunteering events. The training slides say to **prioritize**:

- Events at the **very beginning of the year** (September / early October) or the **very end** (April)
- **Filling out all three event slots**
- Your **most important** events of the year (Nationals and the like)

### What goes in overhead

Per the training slides, overhead is for:

- **Equipment intended to be used ONLY FOR THIS YEAR**
- **Consumable items repurchased every year**
- *"Keeping the lights on"*
- **Year-long rental fees for practice spaces**
- **Group membership dues** necessary to compete or perform (group-level, not individual)
- **Coaching sessions**

## How to submit

1. Decide the events your group will run next year. Look through past budgets; reflect on what you actually
   ran; brainstorm new programming.
2. On **WUGO** (Presence), go to **Forms** and find the **"FY__ Budget Submission Form."**
3. Fill it out as thoroughly as possible. The form holds **three events and an overhead section**.
4. **List events in priority order.**
5. Make sure the **itemized** budget is on the WUGO form itself.
6. Submit.
7. If your BAT leader emails you, **respond promptly**.

You must be listed as a **President or Treasurer of a Category I group in WUGO** to participate — so **keep
your WUGO roster current** well before the cycle opens. After submitting you get an **automated email from
Presence** with a copy of your submission. Keep it.

**You can edit your submission after submitting it** — but only until the deadline, at which point WUGO
access is switched off. Submit early and refine.

## Itemization is a funding condition, not a formality

**Every event and line item must contain the specific item, unit price, and unit quantity.** Budget
Committee **will not fund** events or line items where itemization is insufficient. You are also
responsible for communicating anything *not* captured on the form to your BAT leaders.

The BAM's own examples:

| Good itemization | Bad itemization |
|---|---|
| `Holiday Inn: 1 room x 2 nights (4 people per room): $218.95` | `Hotel: $219` |
| `$1.20 x 3 Lemons from Schnucks: $3.60` | `Lemons and Apples: $15` |
| `$1.24 x 10 Apples from Schnucks: $12.40` | |

Budget Committee also weighs your **past spending** — history of programming and actual spend.

## Placeholders: allowed, but under heightened scrutiny

An event is a **placeholder** if the group can't provide specific logistical cost detail (gas and car
rental with a known location, competition entry fees, practice fees, overhead). BC **prioritizes** events
with clear, specific, itemized costs.

Placeholder requests **may** be approved under heightened scrutiny, at BC's discretion, where the group can
show:

1. **Spending consistency** — repeated participation in the same event and comparable expenses in previous
   years (annual tournaments, national conferences).
2. **Demonstrated need and timing constraints** — a genuine, unavoidable reason beyond the group's control,
   such as short-notice competitions or external timelines requiring rapid commitment.

**The placeholder rule does not apply** to events taking place **before October 1**, nor to events between
**the end of winter break and February 14**. Contact budget@su.wustl.edu for case-by-case guidance.

> The Budget Committee Chair's own words: *"This is a hard deadline. I will be shutting off access to WUGO
> when the deadline hits, and you will no longer be able to edit or submit them after this date has passed.
> No exceptions under any circumstances will be made."*

Expect a similar mid-January open and early-February due date each year, and **confirm the current dates**
with budget@su.wustl.edu.

## After you submit

1. Your **Budget Allocation Team (BAT)** leaders — the coordinators between your group and the Budget
   Committee — email you with any questions. **Answer thoroughly and fast.** The BAM says *"the group's
   budget will not be reviewed without further clarification"*; the training slides say instead that if you
   don't respond *"your budget will be reviewed without the clarifications — this will not trend in your
   favor."* Either way, silence costs you money.
2. Identify your BAT assignment from the **BAM** (the FY27 card text says page four; the FY27 BAM body says
   page five). The FY27 training slides also publish the **BAT Team Assignments** sheet directly:
   `docs.google.com/spreadsheets/d/1XViNiRUESfuxLa7W2PsX9kGlvoH_6yuLWrkCGDuLTCA` — useful when your group
   type overlaps two teams.
3. Budget Committee reviews budgets over roughly six weeks (FY27: February 18 – March 28).
4. Allocations are completed and released as **a spreadsheet of all allocations**, in the same format as the
   Student Activities Fee Mastersheet.
5. The Budget Committee holds **post-allocation office hours**; you can also request an appointment with
   the Budget Committee Chair.

|---|---|

| BAT 2 — bat2@su.wustl.edu | Performing Arts & Media |

| BAT 4 — bat4@su.wustl.edu | Culture, Identity & Belief |

> ⚠️ **Do you get a meeting? The board says both.** The **Annual Budgeting Process** card says *"members of
> the Budget Committee of SU Treasury will meet with each group to get a better understanding of the nature
> of each request."* The FY27 BAM says the opposite as a headline change: *"Student groups will no longer
> be meeting BAT team leaders. All information used to determine budget allocations will be provided by

> **Practical read: write the form as if it's your only chance to be heard, and ask for a meeting if you
> want one.** Confirm with budget@su.wustl.edu.

## Funding limits Budget Committee applies

These are the **BAM / budget-cycle** limits. They are **not** the same numbers as the fiscal-year
Financial Policies used at appeal time — see the callout below.

| Line item | BC limit |
|---|---|
| **Mission events** | Must directly support the group's mission |
| **SU-provided supplies** | **$0** — use the SU office stock (printing, markers, pens, pencils, colored paper, buttons, poster boards) |
| **Merchandise / apparel** | **$0** — buy it with fundraising money |
| **Giveaways / prizes** | **$0** |
| **Food** | Funded **only if critical to the event** (e.g. cultural food for cultural groups). **Not** funded for dry runs, day-long events, as an attendance incentive, or for non-WashU-undergraduate attendees (they don't pay the SAF). Wilderness-mission groups are funded for food. |
| **Plates and cutlery** | **$0** — free compostable ware from the Student Sustainability Board |
| **Speakers & panels** | **$0 — not funded by Budget Committee at all.** You must **appeal** for a speaker or panel during the year. Includes comedians. |
| **Conferences** | **Max 4 members and $3,000 per group.** Networking may only be a *secondary* rationale |
| **Retreats** | **One** retreat, **$100 / person** |
| **Travel cap** | **$682 per active travelling member**, all travel events combined |
| **Flights** | **$350 / person round trip**, and only for events outside a 500-mile radius |
| **500+ mile travel** | **Maximum two** travel events over 500 miles. Inside 500 miles, BC funds driving, not flights |
| **Hotels** | **$150 / room / night**, max **4** students per room |
| **Rental cars** | Sedans **$70/day**, SUVs **$80/day**, Vans **$90/day**; 5 people per car |
| **Gas** | **$0.15 / mile / car** |
| **Buses** | Up to the equivalent cost of rental cars and gas; an extra hotel night may be included if necessary |
| **Individual membership fees** | **Not funded** — not even if required to participate in a particular event. Use dues, fundraising, gifts, or the SU Opportunity Fund |
| **League fees** | Funded where individual members get no direct benefit and the fee supports the club |
| **Athletic trainers / coaches** | Funded for home competitions **only if league- or university-mandated** |
| **Practice space rentals** | Funded only where an SU-funded on-campus space can't do the same job |
| **Clean-up fees** | **Don't budget for them** — the **HES fund** replaces the clean-up fee |

### Socials in the budget cycle

**Social events are not funded by Budget Committee** — *"unless the event takes place before October 1st."*
For one qualifying pre-October-1 large-scale social, BC funds up to:

| Rule | BC limit |
|---|---|
| Per person | **$50 / WashU attendee** |
| Food | **$20 / WashU attendee** |
| Total cost | **< $5,000** |
| Frequency | **One** large-scale social per year, and only if it's before October 1 |

BC venue funding by WashU attendance:

| Attendees | Venue funding |
|---|---|
| < 140 | **$0** — hold it on campus (Holmes Lounge; cleanup is $0 thanks to the HES fund) |
| 140–200 | $2,500 |
| 200–300 | $4,000 |
| 300+ | $5,000 |

SU will not fund individuals who are not active members. Group-wide social events for **cultural,
diversity & inclusion, and affinity groups may be considered mission-related** and therefore **do not count
toward the social cap** — argue this rather than accepting the cap. For socials under 140 attendees,
Treasury funds an on-campus event, with case-by-case exceptions for cultural or religious reasons.

> ⚠️ **Budget-cycle caps and fiscal-year caps disagree, and both are live.** Examples: travel cap **$682**
> (BAM) vs **$712** (FY27 Financial Policies); flights **$350** (BAM) vs **$300/$400** by lead time
> (Policies); hotels **$150** (BAM) vs **$150/$300** by lead time (Policies); gas **$0.15/mile** (BAM) vs
> **$0.20/mile** (Policies); conferences **$3,000 / max 4 members** (BAM) vs **$3,500 / no attendee limit**
> (Policies); socials **$50/person, <$5,000** (BAM) vs **$40/person, ≤$7,000** (Policies). The Financial
> Policies card says it covers Cat I groups "who budget **and** appeal," so this isn't a clean split.
> **Working assumption: use the BAM for a spring budget submission and the Financial Policies for an
> in-year appeal — but confirm.** Budget caps: budget@su.wustl.edu. Policy caps: finance@su.wustl.edu.
> See [appeals.md](appeals.md) and [financial-policies.md](financial-policies.md).

## Who the SAF covers

The Undergraduate **Student Activities Fee funds undergraduate WashU students only**. Budget Committee does
**not** fund any items for **graduate students or coaches**. Students in **3-2 or dual-degree programs are
covered** by the SAF.

## Finality and re-appeal

> Once Treasury approves the budgets after post-allocation office hours, **the Budget Committee's decisions
> stand** — unless:
> - the committee's **rationale explicitly says the group may re-appeal**, or
> - **a circumstance that was the crux of the denial has changed.**

Read the rationale carefully; it's the only place your re-appeal rights are granted. Note this interacts
with the rolling-appeal rule: supplementing an event **is** allowed if that event was last funded in your
**annual budget**.

## When the money arrives

Allocations deposit **automatically** into your Operating account — you don't have to do anything — with
split lifespans:

- **Overhead** funds — available all school year, or until spent
- **Event** funds — available **only** for that specific event; **close out once the event's expenses are
  processed**

Operating funds are **use-it-or-lose-it** and reset to $0 at fiscal year end, and the account may **not**
carry a negative balance. See [accounts.md](accounts.md).

> **Leftovers are swept twice a year.** Per the Operating rules card: *"any unused funds from completed
> events are automatically returned to our general funding pool twice a year (at the end of the Fall and
> Spring semesters)."* And "use it or lose it" is **not** licence to spend down on random items — funds
> must be used for their originally intended purpose. If you have room, ask Business Operations about
> pivoting it to something approved.

## Practical prep

The best predictor of a good allocation is **defensible cost estimates**. Keep actuals from this year's
events in `orgs/<slug>/budget/` — a real past cost is the most credible number you can put in front of the
committee, it's what "spending consistency" means for a placeholder request, and it's what next year's you
will wish you had written down. Prior-year `FY__` daily-report spreadsheets on the board are a second
source for past actuals; see [trello-board.md](trello-board.md).
