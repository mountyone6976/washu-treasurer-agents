# Rolling appeals

For funding a need that arises during the year — an event or item **not included in your budget**.

> **Category I groups only** may submit financial appeals throughout the school year.

## How to submit — through WUGO, not email

**Appeals are filed in WUGO**, not by emailing anyone:

> **wugo.wustl.edu → Forms → SU Financial Appeals**

The Rolling Appeals card has an attached document with screenshots of the submission flow. The email
addresses below are for *questions*; the appeal itself goes through WUGO.

**Put the event start date on the appeal** — that's the date used to judge whether you filed far enough
ahead, and it determines when your funds need to be available.

## Routing, deadlines, and turnaround — by dollar amount

| | **Under $1,000** | **$1,000 or more** |
|---|---|---|
| **Must submit** | **≥ 3 weeks in advance** | **≥ 4 weeks in advance** |
| **Decided by** | **VP of Finance** | **Speaker of the Treasury** |
| **Process** | VPF reviews and comes back to the treasurer with questions before deciding | Speaker routes it through **Forms Teams (FT)** or **Direct to Treasury Appeals (DTA)** depending on complexity and nuance |
| **Turnaround** | **~1 week** | **~2–4 weeks** |
| **Questions to** | finance@su.wustl.edu | treasury@su.wustl.edu |

**Work backwards from the event.** A $1,200 appeal needs 4 weeks' lead *plus* up to 4 weeks of turnaround —
so start **8 weeks out** if you want the money in hand comfortably before you have to spend it. Executive
appeals go to the VP of Finance.

> **SU does not approve appeals retroactively** for money a group has already spent. And approval must land
> **before** you spend or sign anything — if you purchase or a vendor contract is signed first, the new funds
> **cannot** cover it, and the group *or you personally* is responsible for the bill.

If approved, the money is deposited into your **Operating** account.

## Before you write anything — the disqualifier checks

Run these first. Each one kills an appeal outright:

1. **Are you Category I?** If not, appeals aren't available to you — use flat funding, fundraising, or gifts.
2. **Have you already spent or committed the money?** **Deficit spending is not funded.** SU will not fund
   items a group has already spent or committed funds toward, and will not reimburse expenses incurred
   before funding was available. **No exceptions.** The appeal must come first.
3. **Is this a re-appeal?** Re-appeals and rolling appeals **to supplement previous funding are not
   accepted** — *unless* the event was last funded in your **annual budget**. Supplementing a prior *appeal*
   is not allowed.
4. **Is it a GBM?** SU does not fund general body meetings via appeals. Use flat funding.
5. **Is it restricted?** Personal items, giveaways, individual memberships/dues, and anything on the
   Restrictions on Accounts list. See [restrictions.md](restrictions.md).
6. **Is it over a policy cap?** Caps are **hard cutoffs** — over-cap line items are denied, not trimmed. See
   [financial-policies.md](financial-policies.md).

## ⏰ File 4+ weeks out — it's worth real money twice over

For travel, the caps you're held to **depend on how far ahead you file** — and this is separate from, and
stacks with, the submission deadlines above:

| Line item | Filed **< 4 weeks** out | Filed **4+ weeks** out |
|---|---|---|
| Hotels | $150 / room / night | **$200** / room / night |
| Flights | $300 / round trip | **$400** / round trip |
| Sedans | $60 / day | **$70** / day |
| SUVs | $70 / day | **$80** / day |
| Vans | $80 / day | **$90** / day |

Filing three days earlier can be worth hundreds of dollars. This is the single easiest win available to a
treasurer.

## Writing an appeal that lands

Treasury judges against the **SU funding principles** — write in their terms:

- **Need vs. want.** Say why this is a need.
- **Mission-related over social.** Tie the request to your group's mission explicitly. For cultural,
  diversity & inclusion, and affinity groups, group-wide social and formal events **may be considered
  mission-related** and therefore exempt from the social cap — make that argument rather than accepting the
  cap.
- **Line items essential to the mission of the event.** Justify each line on mission grounds, not on
  "it would be nice." SU's own example: food to entice attendance at a GBM is unnecessary; supplies for a
  mission-relevant activity are necessary.
- **Fiscal responsibility.** Pre-answer their three questions: Are there local alternatives? Is it a
  league-sanctioned tournament? Has the group done its due diligence to minimize costs?

**Itemize and show your arithmetic.** Where a cap applies, show your per-person or per-unit number sitting
under it. Where you're seeking a cap exception (venue caps for cultural, religious, or safety reasons),
say so explicitly and give the reason — those are granted case by case, and a group that gets one moves to
the **next tier** cap.

**Speaker events over $10,000** require an **interest survey open at least 5 days**, with results
demonstrating interest and expected attendance. Start it early; it's a precondition.

## Where appeal money lands

Approved appeal funds deposit **automatically** into your Operating account. Like event budget allocations,
**appeal funds are locked to that specific request** and close out once its expenses process.

## Cancelling an event you appealed for

File an **FTF canceled event retrieval** so the funds can be retrieved and reused for other groups' appeals.
Report it promptly.

## Related but different

- **Reallocation** — moving *already-allocated* money to a different purpose. Not an appeal. Between lines of
  the same event: handled by Business Ops at spending time. Between different events: FTF, decided by the
  VP of Finance.
- **Annual budget** — the spring cycle for next year's three largest events plus overhead. See
  [annual-budget.md](annual-budget.md).

## Keep your own record

Log every appeal and its outcome in `orgs/<slug>/appeals/`, including denials and the rationale given.
Rationale text tells you what Treasury actually weighs, and re-appeal eligibility depends on knowing how
something was funded last time.
