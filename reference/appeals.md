# Rolling appeals

For funding a need that arises during the year — an event or item **not included in your budget**.

> **Category I groups only** may submit financial appeals throughout the school year. Confirmed on the
> live **Rolling Appeals Process** card and on the Category II / Category III cards, both of which list
> appeals under "Do not have access to."

## How to submit — through WUGO, not email

**Appeals are filed in WUGO**, not by emailing anyone:

> **wugo.wustl.edu → Forms → SU Financial Appeals**

The email addresses below are for *questions*; the appeal itself goes through WUGO.

> **The screenshot walkthrough is missing.** The Rolling Appeals checklist says *"Please review the
> attached document for images of the process of submitting an appeal in WUGO"* — but the card currently
> carries **zero attachments**. If you want the visual walkthrough, ask finance@su.wustl.edu for it.

**Put the event start date on the appeal** — that's the date used to judge whether you filed far enough
ahead, and it determines when your funds need to be available.

**Your appeal must adhere to the Financial Policies.** The Rolling Appeals checklist links straight to the
Financial Policies card for this reason. Check every line against
[financial-policies.md](financial-policies.md) before you submit.

## Routing, deadlines, and turnaround — by dollar amount

| | **Under $1,000** | **$1,000 or more** |
|---|---|---|
| **Must submit** | **≥ 3 weeks in advance** | **≥ 4 weeks in advance** |
| **Decided by** | **VP of Finance** | **Speaker of the Treasury** |
| **Process** | VPF reviews and comes back to the treasurer with questions before deciding | Speaker routes it through **Forms Teams (FT)** or **Direct to Treasury Appeals (DTA)** depending on complexity and nuance |
| **Turnaround** | **~1 week** | **~2–4 weeks** |
| **Questions to** | finance@su.wustl.edu | treasury@su.wustl.edu |

**Work backwards from the event.** A $1,200 appeal needs 4 weeks' lead *plus* up to 4 weeks of turnaround —
so start **8 weeks out** if you want the money in hand comfortably before you have to spend it.

**Executive appeals** are a separate route, handled by the **VP of Finance** (finance@su.wustl.edu). The
FY27 General Budget funds *Student Union Executive Appeals* as its own line — see
[funding-sources.md](funding-sources.md) for the amount.

> **SU does not approve appeals retroactively** for money a group has already spent. And approval must land
> **before** you spend or sign anything — if you purchase or a vendor contract is signed first, the new funds
> **cannot** cover it, and the group *or you personally* is responsible for the bill.

If approved, the money is deposited into your **Operating** account.

## Before you write anything — the disqualifier checks

Run these first. Each one kills an appeal outright:

1. **Are you Category I?** If not, appeals aren't available to you — use flat funding, fundraising, or gifts.
2. **Have you already spent or committed the money?** **Deficit spending is not funded.** SU will not fund
   items a group has already spent or committed funds toward, and will not reimburse expenses incurred
   before funding was available. **No exceptions.** The appeal must come first.
3. **Is this a re-appeal?** Re-appeals and rolling appeals **to supplement previous funding are not
   accepted** — *unless* the event was last funded in your **annual budget**. Supplementing a prior *appeal*
   is not allowed.
4. **Is it a GBM?** *"Student Union will continue its practice to not fund General Body Meetings (GBMs) via
   appeals."* Use flat funding.
5. **Is it restricted?** Personal items, giveaways, individual memberships/dues, and anything on the
   Restrictions on Accounts list. See [restrictions.md](restrictions.md).
   > The board is inconsistent on individual memberships. The **SU Funding Principles** card restricts
   > "individual memberships or dues" *"(unless required to compete)"*; the **FY27 Financial Policies** card
   > funds only membership costs *"required to keep an organization in compliance with their governing
   > body"*; the **FY27 BAM** says SU *"will not fund membership costs that are associated with any single
   > student even if required to participate in a particular event."* Don't appeal for an individual
   > membership without clearing it with finance@su.wustl.edu first. Tournament **registration** fees are a
   > separate scheme and are unaffected.
6. **Is it over a policy cap?** Caps are **hard cutoffs** — over-cap line items are denied, not trimmed. See
   [financial-policies.md](financial-policies.md).

## ⏰ File 4+ weeks out — it's worth real money twice over

For travel, the caps you're held to **depend on how far ahead of the event date you file** — and this is
separate from, and stacks with, the submission deadlines above.

**The Travel Cap: $712 per active travelling member** for the FY27 year, set equal to the Student
Activities Fee (SAF).

| Line item | Appeal filed **< 4 weeks** from event date | Appeal filed **4+ weeks** from event date |
|---|---|---|
| Hotels | $150 / room / night | **$300** / room / night |
| Flights | $300 / round trip | **$400** / round trip |
| Sedans | $65 / day | **$75** / day |
| SUVs | $75 / day | **$85** / day |
| Vans | $85 / day | **$95** / day |
| Gas | $0.20 / mile | $0.20 / mile |

Filing three days earlier can be worth hundreds of dollars — the hotel cap alone **doubles**. This is the
single easiest win available to a treasurer.

Also: travel **under 500 miles** is funded **as if the group is driving**, and **driving is not permitted
for travel 500+ miles**. Hotel-cap exceptions may be granted by Treasury case by case **if the hotel venue
is required by the event host**.

> ⚠️ **These are the FY27 figures from the live Financial Policies card**, which is labelled
> *"FY27 Financial Policies."* Where [financial-policies.md](financial-policies.md) and
> [travel.md](travel.md) still show FY26 numbers (notably a $200 hotel cap and a $682 travel cap), **the
> board wins** and those files need updating.

> ⚠️ **Two different travel caps are live on the board.** The Financial Policies card says **$712** per
> active travelling member; the **FY27 Budget Allocation Manual** and its executive summary say **$682**,
> and both call their figure "equivalent to the SAF." **Working assumption — not stated anywhere on the
> board:** the BAM caps govern what Budget Committee allocates in the spring cycle, and the Financial
> Policies caps govern in-year appeals. **Confirm which applies to your request with
> finance@su.wustl.edu.** See [annual-budget.md](annual-budget.md) for the BAM's separate, and generally
> lower, travel line-item caps.

## Writing an appeal that lands

Treasury judges against the **SU funding principles** — write in their terms:

- **Need vs. want.** Say why this is a need.
- **Mission-related over social.** Tie the request to your group's mission explicitly. For cultural,
  diversity & inclusion, and affinity groups, group-wide social and formal events **may be considered
  mission-related** and therefore do not count toward the social cap — make that argument rather than
  accepting the cap.
- **Line items essential to the mission of the event.** Justify each line on mission grounds, not on
  "it would be nice." SU's own example: food used to entice students to attend a GBM is unnecessary;
  supplies for a mission-relevant activity during an event are necessary.
- **Fiscal responsibility.** Pre-answer their three questions: Are there local alternatives? Is it a
  league-sanctioned tournament? Has the group done its due diligence to minimize costs?

**Itemize and show your arithmetic.** Where a cap applies, show your per-person or per-unit number sitting
under it. Where you're seeking a cap exception (venue caps for cultural, religious, or safety reasons),
say so explicitly and give the reason — those are granted case by case, and a group that gets one moves to
the **next tier** cap.

> **"SU's thought process during budgeting or appeals is to fund events up from $0 rather than starting
> with the requested amount and finding ways down from there."** Every line has to earn its place from
> zero. A padded total isn't trimmed proportionally — unjustified lines simply never get added.

### Speaker and guest events

**Honoraria are capped at $10,000 per group annually**, as allocated by Treasury. Groups wanting a speaker
or guest with an honorarium **greater than $10,000** should **contact the Social Programming Board
*before* submitting the appeal**, to ask about additional funding through the **Supplementary Speaker
Fund**.

> **Correction to earlier snapshots of this file.** The requirement for an *interest survey open ≥ 5 days*
> for speakers over $10,000, and a $25,000 annual honoraria cap, **appear nowhere on the current board** —
> not in any description and not in any checklist. Don't plan around them. Confirm with
> finance@su.wustl.edu if a large speaker event is in play.

## After approval — attendance tracking

Approval comes with a reporting obligation. **Attendance must be tracked** for:

- all events with an allocation **greater than $10,000**,
- all **speaker events greater than $1,000**, and
- all **travel events** (excluding local off-campus travel).

Acceptable methods are **SA Tools** or **reported overall ticket sales**. Attendance tracking is encouraged
for all SU-funded events. Full policy in [financial-policies.md](financial-policies.md).

## Where appeal money lands

Approved appeal funds deposit **automatically** into your Operating account — you don't have to do
anything. Like event budget allocations, **appeal funds are available only for that specific request** and
**close out once the expenses are processed**.

> **Unused money doesn't sit there.** Per the Operating rules card, *"any unused funds from completed
> events are automatically returned to our general funding pool twice a year (at the end of the Fall and
> Spring semesters)."* Plan to spend an approved appeal within its semester.

Your Operating account is **not permitted to carry a negative balance**.

## Cancelling an event you appealed for

File an **FTF canceled event retrieval** so the funds can be retrieved and reused for other groups' appeals.
"Canceled Event Retrievals" is one of the five things the Financial Transaction Form exists for. Report it
promptly. See [ftf.md](ftf.md).

## Related but different

- **Reallocation** — moving *already-allocated* money to a different purpose. Not an appeal.
  - **Minor reallocations (between lines of the *same* event)** are handled **at the discretion of the
    Business Staff during spending** — no separate approval cycle.
  - **Anything else — notably reallocating between *different* events** — is reported on the **Financial
    Transaction Form** and **handled by the VP of Finance**.
  - Either way: **groups should not spend over what they were allocated** for a line item or event, and a
    reallocation must be settled before the money moves.
- **Annual budget** — the spring cycle for next year's three largest events plus overhead. See
  [annual-budget.md](annual-budget.md).
- **Flat funding** — your unrestricted annual allocation, and the right tool for GBMs, socials, info
  sessions, and topping up an under-funded event. See [funding-sources.md](funding-sources.md).

## Keep your own record

Log every appeal and its outcome in `orgs/<slug>/appeals/`, including denials and the rationale given.
Rationale text tells you what Treasury actually weighs, and re-appeal eligibility depends on knowing how
something was funded last time.
