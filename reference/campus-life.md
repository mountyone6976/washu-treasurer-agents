# Campus Life — the non-money approvals

Campus Life controls **whether your event may happen**. SU controls **whether SU money pays for it**. Two
separate gates; you need both.

> **Sources:** the BEARings *Campus Life/University Policies and the 2026-2027 Student Group Handbook* card
> ([trello.com/c/eBYmRw9i](https://trello.com/c/eBYmRw9i)) and its attachment
> `2026-2027_Student_Group_Handbook.pdf`, checked **2026-09-04**.

## WUGO event registration

- **All programs, events and activities must be registered in WUGO at least 14 days in advance.** Both the
  card and the handbook say 14 days.
- **"Event" is defined broadly.** The handbook's definition of a *Program, Event, or Activity* is "any
  organized and structured undertaking planned, executed, or attended by a student group," and it
  enumerates: **general body meetings, executive meetings, tryouts, rehearsals, practices, meets, games,
  workshops, conferences, performances, community service projects, volunteering, and other interactive
  experiences.** So yes — practices and GBMs count.
- Registration is **Campus Life policy, not an SU finance rule**, and it must be **approved**, not just
  submitted.

### Spending before your event is approved

**The 2026–2027 Student Group Handbook is unambiguous:** *"SU recognized student groups may not spend or
incur expenses until their program, event or activity has been fully approved in WUGO."*

> ⚠️ **This conflicts with an earlier reading of the process that circulated in this repo** — namely that
> Business Operations no longer requires proof of WUGO registration before spending, since the FTF stopped
> asking about it. Both things can be true at once and that's the trap: **the FTF not asking is not the
> same as the policy not applying.** The Campus Life handbook still forbids incurring the expense, and
> Campus Life, not Business Ops, enforces it.
>
> **Treat "fully approved in WUGO" as a precondition for spending.** If you need to move before approval,
> get that in writing from your Campus Life Advisor *and* your Business Coordinator first.

> **On the "repayment rule":** guidance has circulated saying that if funds are spent on an event that is
> later denied, the group must repay them and may make no further purchases until it does. **That sentence
> appears nowhere on the current open BEARings board or in the 2026–2027 handbook**, so it is recorded here
> as unverified rather than as policy. What *is* on the board is the compliance-pause list — card use,
> reimbursements, supplier payments, deposits and reallocations all stop while a group is out of compliance
> (see [treasurer-role.md](treasurer-role.md)) — which has the same practical effect. Confirm the specifics
> with your Business Coordinator before relying on either version.

### High-risk events get more scrutiny

The handbook defines a **high risk program, event or activity** as one involving **any** of: **alcohol**,
**over 100 attendees**, **non-WashU students/staff/faculty**, an **outdoor** setting, an **off-campus**
setting, or **participants or guests under 18**. If your event trips any of these, book time with your
advisor early — this is exactly the category where a late WUGO registration gets denied.

## Your Campus Life Advisor

Every recognized undergraduate group is assigned one. They advise on group development, event planning,
financial management, risk management and leadership, and they act as **liaison to university
administration**. They are the right person for whether and how an event can happen.

- **Be proactive** — schedule a meeting **early in your planning phase**, not once you're committed.
- **Keep them fully updated.** Share event changes and updates **even after the event has been officially
  approved.**
- **Contracts: you cannot sign.** The handbook is explicit — *"Student groups do not have the authority to
  sign contracts or enter into agreements on behalf of the university or group. Instead, these agreements
  must be signed by a Campus Life Advisor."* Route every performer, vendor and venue agreement through your
  advisor.

> SU's stated golden rule: following Campus Life and university policies is the best way to get your
> registered events approved without delay.

**Your faculty/staff advisor is a different person** — selected by your group, not by Campus Life, and
required for some categories. Check whether yours is required in the **WUGO Admin Portal → Groups → your
group → Details tab → "Advisor Required."** Faculty/staff advisors sign a **new advisor agreement each
academic year**, after which they appear on your WUGO roster. And note the handbook's boundary list:
advisors **don't** make group decisions, run meetings, plan events, **or create budgets**.

## Membership, roster and re-registration

These are compliance requirements, and failing them is a conduct matter — not just an inconvenience.

- **Minimum 10 members at all times**, with **at least a president and a treasurer** listed as officers.
  For new-group recognition the handbook adds that president and treasurer **cannot be the same person**.
- **Roster must be up to date in WUGO at all times.**
- Membership is limited to **enrolled WashU undergraduate or graduate students**. **Graduate students are
  prohibited from serving as an officer or executive of an undergraduate student group.**
- Students **studying abroad cannot hold an officer position**. Students **on a leave of absence** (medical,
  mental health, academic, family, personal or other) **are not eligible to participate** in a registered
  student group, hold a leadership position, or attend/plan a group event as a representative.
- **Failure to maintain membership requirements may result in a student group conduct referral.**
- **Re-registration happens every spring** in WUGO. Minimum requirements: 10 active members on the roster,
  at least one president and one treasurer, and **at least one registered *and approved* program, event or
  activity during Aug 29, 2026 – May 3, 2027.** Miss the spring deadline and the group is **deactivated for
  the next academic year** and must reapply as a brand-new group in the fall.

Travel FTFs also lean on the roster: they ask you to upload the trip roster and to name any **coach,
graduate students, or individuals not on the WUGO roster** who are travelling. See [travel.md](travel.md).

## Money rules that Campus Life enforces too

From the handbook's *Student Group Finances* page — these overlap SU policy, and Campus Life will hold you
to them independently:

- **No outside bank accounts.** SU-recognized groups may not maintain accounts outside the SU Business
  Office; all funds stay in your SU accounts.
- **Collections only via cash, check, or CashNet.** **Venmo, PayPal and other third-party providers are not
  permitted.**
- **Gifts or donations from outside the group** — a department, sponsor or individual — require working
  with your **SU Business Coordinator in advance**. See
  [fundraising-and-gifts.md](fundraising-and-gifts.md).
- **The treasurer must physically be present at the SU Business Office** to manage transactions using SU
  funds. See the conflict flagged in [treasurer-role.md](treasurer-role.md).
