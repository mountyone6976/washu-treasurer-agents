> **"FY27 Financial Policies"** (verified against the live board on **2026-09-04**), so the FY26 numbers
> this file used to carry are **superseded**. Every figure below is quoted from that card unless it is
> explicitly labelled *BAM*. Card: <https://trello.com/c/E5bZT3Ex> — re-verify any material number with

> ⚠️ **Two sets of FY27 numbers exist and they disagree.** The **FY27 Budget Allocation Manual** (BAM,
> written by the Budget Committee Chair in **Spring 2026**, before Treasury approved FY27 policies) and its
> linked *Funding Limits Executive Summary* still carry older, mostly tighter figures: travel cap **$682**,
> flights **$350** round trip, gas **$0.15/mile**, conferences **$3,000 and max 4 members**, socials
> **$5,000 total / $50 per person / food $20 per person**, venue tiers topping out at **$5,000**. Those are

> what governs **appeals during this year**. Where a number matters, confirm which document applies:
> **finance@su.wustl.edu** for appeals and policy, **budget@su.wustl.edu** for the annual budget.

**Scope: these policies apply to Category I groups that budget and appeal for events.** Cat II and Cat III
get flat funding only — see [categories.md](categories.md) and [funding-sources.md](funding-sources.md).

Caps are **hard cutoffs** — a line item over the limit isn't trimmed, it's denied.

## Attendance tracking — new in FY27

Attendance **must** be tracked for:

| Trigger | |
|---|---|
| Any event with an allocation | **greater than $10,000** |
| Any **speaker event** | **greater than $1,000** |
| **All travel events** | excluding **local off-campus** travel |

**Acceptable methods:** use of **SA Tools**, or **reported overall ticket sales**. Attendance tracking is
**encouraged for all SU-funded events** — and since it is now a condition on the largest allocations, plan
the mechanism *before* the event, not after.

## Socials

Applies to events categorized **"Social (non-mission)."**

| Rule | Limit |
|---|---|
| Total cost per social event | **≤ $7,000** |
| Per-person cost | **≤ $40/person** |
| Frequency | **One (1) social event per fiscal year** |
| Attendee mix | **≥ 60% of attendees must be group members** |
| Location | Must occur in a **single location** |

**Off-campus venue funding by attendance:**

| Attendees | Venue funding |
|---|---|
| < 140 | **$0** — groups may use an **on-campus space** instead |
| 140–199 | $4,000 |
| 200–299 | $5,500 |
| 300+ | $7,000 |

**Exceptions:** venue-cap exceptions are granted by **Treasury**, **case by case, for cultural, religious,
or safety reasons**; a group granted one is **subject to the next tier cap**.

**Important carve-out:** group-wide social **and formal** events for **cultural, diversity & inclusion, and
affinity groups may be considered mission-related**, and therefore **do not count toward the social cap**.
If your group is one of these, argue mission-relatedness rather than accepting the social cap by default.

**GBMs are never funded via appeals** — "Student Union will continue its practice to not fund General Body
Meetings (GBMs) via appeals." Use flat funding.

> **No food-per-person cap appears in the FY27 policies.** The **$20/person food cap** exists only in the
> FY27 BAM, as a *budget-round* limit. Whether it still binds an appeal is not stated on the board —
> confirm with finance@su.wustl.edu before assuming either way.

## Honoraria (speakers and guests)

| Rule | Limit |
|---|---|
| Total honoraria per group per year | **≤ $10,000, as allocated by Treasury** |

Above that ceiling the card points groups elsewhere rather than raising it — quoting the card: *"Groups
interested in hosting speaker or guest events with an honorarium greater than $10,000 should contact the
Social Programming Board before submitting their appeal to inquire about additional funding through the
**Supplementary Speaker Fund**."* Note the sequence: **that conversation happens before the appeal is
submitted.**

> **Two FY26 rules are gone.** The FY26 honoraria ceiling of **$25,000**, the limit of **one speaker over
> $10,000**, and the **≥ 5-day interest survey** requirement no longer appear anywhere on the live board
> (descriptions or checklists). Treat them as withdrawn, but expect to evidence attendance instead:
> **speaker events over $1,000 now require attendance tracking.** If an appeal reviewer asks for an
> interest survey anyway, confirm with finance@su.wustl.edu.

## Travel

**The Travel Cap: $712 per active travelling member** — set equal to the FY27 **Student Activities Fee
(SAF)**, up from $682 in FY26.

**Lead time changes your caps. Filing 4+ weeks out is worth real money:**

| Line item | Appeal filed **< 4 weeks** out | Appeal filed **4+ weeks** out |
|---|---|---|
| Hotels | $150 / room / night | **$300** / room / night |
| Flights | $300 / round trip | **$400** / round trip |
| Sedans | $65 / day | **$75** / day |
| SUVs | $75 / day | **$85** / day |
| Vans | $85 / day | **$95** / day |
| Gas | $0.20 / mile | $0.20 / mile |

**Hotel exception:** Treasury may grant a case-by-case exception to the hotel cap **if the hotel venue is
required by the event host** for the event.

**Distance rules:**
- Travel **under 500 miles** is funded **as if the group is driving**.
- **Driving is not permitted for travel 500+ miles.**

> ⚠️ **The FY26 fall/spring rule on 500+ mile trips is no longer in the policies.** The FY27 card says
> nothing about how many 500+ mile trips a group may take; the **FY27 BAM** caps *budgets* at "a maximum of
> two travel events over 500 miles." No spring/fall split appears anywhere on the current board. Don't
> plan a second long trip on the strength of the old rule — confirm with finance@su.wustl.edu.

[travel.md](travel.md) carries the same FY27 caps and the same conflict callout — both files were updated
together on 2026-09-04.

## Retreats

**Definition (FY27, new):** a retreat is *"a structured event where a student group steps away from typical
activities to focus on growth, planning, team development, collaboration, and/or relationship building."*

- **≤ $100 per person.**
- If the retreat is determined to be a **travel event**, it is **also subject to the travel cap**.

## Conferences

**Definition (FY27, new):** an event providing *"training, resources, or growth opportunities that can be
brought back and applied to improve student group operations, programs, leadership development, or member
engagement."* **Attendance must primarily benefit the organization and its members as a whole**, not the
personal or professional development of individual attendees — write the appeal in those terms.

| Rule | Limit |
|---|---|
| **Annual cap for conference travel** | **$3,500** |
| Number of attendees | **No limit** |
| If it's a travel event | **The travel cap also applies** |

> ⚠️ **Conflict with the BAM.** The FY27 BAM caps conferences at **$3,000 and a maximum of four members**;
> the FY27 policies raise the cap to **$3,500 with no attendee limit**. For appeals, use the card. If your
> conference sits inside an already-allocated annual budget line, you were funded under the BAM version.
> Confirm with finance@su.wustl.edu.

> **Which line items count against the conference cap is no longer documented.** The FY26 breakdown
> (Lodging, Registration/Membership Fee, Transportation) is not on the current board. Ask
> finance@su.wustl.edu rather than assuming.

## Individual memberships

SU funds **only** membership costs **required to keep the organization in compliance with its governing
body**.

**FY27 wording, and it is a real loosening:** *"Individual memberships will only be paid if individual
members do not receive direct benefits from membership (such as a national organization requiring two
memberships in order to keep the entire student group/team in compliance)."* So the test is **direct
individual benefit**, not merely whether the membership is held by an individual.

This does not change how **registration fees for specific tournaments** are funded. For individual
organizational membership fees, use **dues, fundraising, gifts, or the SU Opportunity Fund** (if eligible).

> The **FY27 BAM** states flatly that BC "does not fund individual membership fees for national
> organizations" in budget submissions. The card's carve-out is the appeal-side rule.

## Uniforms

Funded only if **both** are true:
1. **Required to participate in an event**, and
2. Have a **useful life of more than one year**.

## Revenue from SU-funded events

- **80% of revenue returns to SU**; **20%** goes to your fundraising account.
- If SU's 80% would exceed its principal allocation, the **overage goes to your fundraising account** —
  SU recoups only what it allocated.
- Revenue includes but isn't limited to: ticket sales or similar, **monetary awards** (to the group *or* to
  individual participants), and any other funds generated by hosting or participating in an SU-funded event.
- **New in FY27:** if a group runs a fundraiser out of its **fundraising or gift** accounts, it **keeps 100%
  of the revenue generated.** The 80/20 split attaches to *SU-funded* events only. See
  [fundraising-and-gifts.md](fundraising-and-gifts.md).

## How SU thinks about these limits

Straight from the Financial Policies card's "Other Guidelines" checklist:

- These caps describe **limits on what SU will allocate** toward certain items within a fiscal year. Groups
  are free to **supplement SU's funding** with their own fundraising, flat funding, or gifted funds beyond
  what SU provides.
- They apply **only to Category I groups**, the only ones that budget and appeal for events.
- **Anything not explicitly listed above is not subject to any specific funding limit** — it's judged on the
  funding principles instead (see [restrictions.md](restrictions.md)).
- > **"SU's thought process during budgeting or appeals is to fund events up from $0 rather than starting
  > with the requested amount and finding ways down from there."**

That last point should shape how you write requests: every line has to earn its place from zero. A padded
total isn't trimmed proportionally — unjustified lines simply never get added.

## Paying students for services

**$0.** From the FY27 card's checklist: *"Paying Students for services provided to a group: $0. This year,
we cannot pay students for services due to issues with payroll through Workday. Students that are not
student employees may be paid."*

> The checklist item carries **no fiscal year of its own** — it sits on the card titled *FY27 Financial
> Policies*, so "this year" reads as FY27, but the wording is inherited from FY26. Combined with the
> all-accounts prohibition on **payments to WashU employees, including student employees**, the practical
> rule is unchanged: **don't plan on paying a student for a service.** Confirm the current position with
> finance@su.wustl.edu before promising anyone anything.

## Other standing rules

- **Re-appeals:** *"re-appeals and rolling appeals to supplement previous funding will not be accepted,
  unless the event was last funded in the group's annual budget."* See [appeals.md](appeals.md).
- **Deficit spending:** SU will not fund items a group has **already spent or committed** funds toward.
- **Restrictions on Accounts:** established by the **VP of Finance with the SU Business Manager**, who may
  **amend it at any point during the year**. **In cases of ambiguity, the VP of Finance decides** whether a
  purchase is restricted. **Any SU-initiated change must be approved by majority vote of the Financial
  Leadership Team.** The list itself: [restrictions.md](restrictions.md).
- **Flat funding (FY27):** **$712 per group** — a flat allocation to **all Category I and Category II
  groups**, equal to the current SAF. See [funding-sources.md](funding-sources.md).
