# Where the money comes from

Three SU funding streams, plus what you raise yourself. All SU awards deposit **automatically** into your
Operating account — *"you don't have to do a thing."*

| Stream | Who's eligible | When | Covers |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Annual budget allocation** | Cat I | Submitted each spring for the *following* fiscal year | Your 3 highest-priority events + overhead |
| **Flat funding** | Cat I & II | Automatic, annual | Almost anything not on the restricted list |
| **Rolling appeals** | Cat I | As needed, throughout the year | Specific events/needs not in your budget |
| **Fundraising / gifts** | All | Any time | See [fundraising-and-gifts.md](fundraising-and-gifts.md) |

Cat III groups get **none** of the three SU streams — see [categories.md](categories.md).

## Flat funding — your flexible money

The most useful money you have, because it's the least restricted.

**FY27: a flat $712 per group for the full academic year**, for **Category I and Category II** groups.
$712 is the current **Student Activities Fee (SAF)**. It is a flat per-*group* allocation — there is no
per-member arithmetic in the FY27 policy.

| Category | FY27 flat funding |
|---|---|
| Cat I | **$712** per group |
| Cat II | **$712** per group |
| Cat III | **None** |

Both the FY27 **Financial Policies** card (*"All Category I and Category II student groups will receive a
flat allocation of $712 (equal to the current Student Activities Fee)"*) and the **Flat Funding** card
(*"$712 per group for the full academic year"*) say the same thing.

> **The SAF figure itself is stated two ways on the board.** The FY27 Financial Policies and Flat Funding
> cards both call the SAF **$712**; the **FY27 Budget Allocation Manual** and its executive summary both
> call it **$682**. Since flat funding is pegged to the SAF, this is a ~$30/group question. Confirm with
> finance@su.wustl.edu.

> ⚠️ **The Category I and Category II cards still describe the old scheme.** Their checklists say *"Flat
> funding of $15 per active member per fiscal year — flat funding is capped for all groups at $1350 per
> fiscal year."* That is **not** what the FY27 policy says. The FY27-labelled Financial Policies card and
> the Flat Funding card are the newer, more specific sources and both say a flat **$712**, so that is what
> this file carries — but the conflict is live on the board. **Confirm your group's actual flat allocation
> with finance@su.wustl.edu** rather than budgeting on either number.

> ⚠️ **Two rules earlier snapshots of this file carried have no support on the current board:** a cap of
> **150% of the previous year's spending**, and a **$150 minimum floor**. Neither phrase appears anywhere
> on the live board — not in any card description, not in any checklist. A flat per-group allocation has no
> room for either. They have been removed. If someone tells you a 150% cap or a $150 floor applies, get it
> confirmed by finance@su.wustl.edu in writing.

**What flat funding can be used for:** any event that doesn't fall under the restricted allocations list —
explicitly including **social events, general body meetings (GBMs), and information sessions**, none of
which SU funds through appeals. It can also **supplement events that were budgeted or appealed for but not
funded to the extent the group was seeking**. *"The purpose of Flat Funding is to provide groups with much
more flexibility."*

It's also the answer to several specific budget-cycle refusals: Budget Committee doesn't fund food for dry
runs or day-long events and points groups to flat funding instead. See
[annual-budget.md](annual-budget.md).

**Lifespan:** flat funding *"lives safely tucked away in your Operating Account and is available for you to
use whenever you need it throughout the entire school year (or until it runs out)."* Like everything else
in Operating, it **resets to $0** at fiscal year end. See [accounts.md](accounts.md).

> ⚠️ **Your flat funding is what HES overages get charged to first.** SU absorbs the regular rate for HES
> set-up / tear-down / clean-up. But if the cost exceeds the regular rate — an **Emergency (less than
> 72-hour notification)** or **Holiday Rate** — *"the student group will be responsible for the overage.
> This additional cost will first be deducted from the student group's Flat Funding in the Operating
> account first, followed by the student group's Fundraising and/or Gift accounts."* SU's General Budget
> covers **no** HES Special Service Request costs. **Book event services more than 72 hours out** or your
> most flexible money pays for it. See [su-resources.md](su-resources.md).

## Annual budget allocation

Submitted in spring for the next fiscal year; covers your **three largest events plus overhead**. **Category
I only.** Full process, the BAM's funding limits, and the FY27 calendar in
[annual-budget.md](annual-budget.md).

Note the split lifespan once it lands: **overhead** money is available all school year (or until spent),
while **event** money is available **only for that specific event** and **closes out once the event's
expenses are processed**.

> **Equipment is contested.** The Annual Budgeting Process card still lists "overhead and equipment," but
> the FY27 BAM says equipment is no longer an accepted budget category. Confirm with budget@su.wustl.edu —
> details in [annual-budget.md](annual-budget.md).

## Rolling appeals

For needs that arise during the year. **Category I only.** Routing depends on size — **under $1,000 is
decided by the VP of Finance** (~1 week turnaround), **$1,000 or more by the Speaker of the Treasury**
(~2–4 weeks). Submission deadlines are **≥ 3 weeks** ahead under $1,000 and **≥ 4 weeks** ahead at $1,000+.
Appeal funds are locked to the specific request and close out when its expenses process. Full process,
including the travel caps that make filing 4+ weeks out worth real money, in [appeals.md](appeals.md).

## The smaller and less obvious pots

Beyond the three main streams, the board names several other routes:

| Fund | For | Route |
|---|---|---|
| **SU Executive Appeals** | Separate appeal track, funded as its own General Budget line | **VP of Finance** — finance@su.wustl.edu |
| **Supplementary Speaker Fund** | Honoraria **above** the $10,000 per-group annual cap | Contact the **Social Programming Board** *before* submitting your appeal |
| **SU Opportunity Fund** | Individual organizational membership fees (if eligible) — which SU otherwise won't fund | Eligibility unclear from the board; ask finance@su.wustl.edu |
| **HES Fund** | Set-up / tear-down / clean-up event services; **replaces the clean-up fee** so groups don't budget for cleaning | Cost bypasses the group and is absorbed by the SU General Budget — but see the warning below |
| **Block Funding** | A General Budget line ("Block Funding Petitions") for a few named programming entities — FY27: Campus Y, EST, Uncle Joe's | The board's open lists document **no application process**; ask finance@su.wustl.edu whether petitioning is open to other groups |

## Where all of it comes from — the FY27 General Budget

The General Budget is the whole SAF pool, balanced by the VP of Finance and approved by Senate and
Treasury. **The FY27 numbers are published** on the board's *FY27 General Budget* card as a readable
spreadsheet. Final approved lines that matter to a student-group treasurer:

| FY27 General Budget line | Funded in FY26 | **FY27 final approved** |
|---|---|---|
| Total estimated revenue | *see note* | **$4,801,408.34** |
| **Student Union Budgets/Appeals** | $2,494,287.93 | **$2,702,971.93** |
| **Student Union Executive Appeals** | $40,000.00 | **$100,000.00** |
| HES Fund | $75,000.00 | **$75,000.00** |
| *Total student allocation boards* | $2,534,287.93 | **$2,802,971.93** |

> The revenue row's prior-year cell reads **$4,651,408.34**, but that column is headed both "Funded in
> FY26" and "BALANCE" in the sheet, so it isn't clear whether it's FY26 total revenue or a balance figure.
> Not guessing — confirm with finance@su.wustl.edu if the comparison matters.

**Student Union Budgets/Appeals** is the pot your annual budget and your rolling appeals are paid out of —
it's also the `PJ000025472 Student Union Budgets/Appeals` counter-entry you'll see on every award line in
the daily reports (see [trello-board.md](trello-board.md)). Executive Appeals **more than doubled** for
FY27, which is worth knowing before you dismiss that route.

> **Money is recycled mid-year.** Per the Operating rules card, *"any unused funds from completed events are
> automatically returned to our general funding pool twice a year (at the end of the Fall and Spring
> semesters)."* Both a reason to spend approved money in its own semester and a reason a late appeal can
> still succeed.

### Tracking allocations as they're made

The **Student Activities Fee Mastersheet 2026-27** card holds a spreadsheet of **all** SAF allocations to
student groups and school councils, *"edited in real time, constantly, as allocations are being made by the
VP of Finance and Student Union Treasury."*

> **Gated.** The Mastersheet is a personal-OneDrive SharePoint link and returns **403** without WashU SSO —
> an agent can't read it. Open it yourself while signed in, from the *Student Activities Fee Mastersheet
> 2026-27* card, or ask finance@su.wustl.edu.

## Who the money is for

The Undergraduate **SAF funds WashU undergraduates only**. SU does not fund items for **graduate students
or coaches**. Students in **3-2 or dual-degree programs are covered**. Costs pertaining to non-WashU or
non-undergraduate students are on the Operating restrictions list — see [restrictions.md](restrictions.md).

## What none of these will do

- **Fund deficit spending.** *"Student Union will not fund items for which student groups have already
  spent or committed funds towards."* Secure the money first — this is the single most expensive mistake a
  treasurer can make.
- **Fund GBMs via appeals.** *"Student Union will continue its practice to not fund General Body Meetings
  (GBMs) via appeals."* Use flat funding.
- **Supplement a prior award via re-appeal**, unless the event was last funded in your annual budget.
- **Let your Operating account go negative.** It is not permitted to fall below $0.
- **Cover you while you're out of compliance.** A compliance pause suspends card use, reimbursements,
  supplier payments, deposits, and reallocations. See [treasurer-role.md](treasurer-role.md).
