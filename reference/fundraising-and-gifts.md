# Fundraising, sponsorships, and gifts

Two accounts, two completely different intake pipelines. **Fundraising money you collect yourself;
gift money you never touch** — it arrives through University Advancement. Getting the wrong pipeline is
the single most common mistake here, because the paperwork that legitimizes one is the wrong paperwork
for the other.

## Fundraising vs. gift — get this distinction right

| | **Fundraising** | **Gift / donation** |
|---|---|---|
| What it is | You sell something, or a sponsor gets something back | A **voluntary transfer of money with no product, service, or tangible benefit expected in return** |
| Routed through | **You collect it** — Cash / Check / Cashnet | **University Advancement** and the central Gift Department |
| Form | **FTF — fundraising request**, before you collect a dollar | **No FTF.** The donor uses Advancement channels |
| Lands in | Fundraising account (or Operating — see below) | Gift account |
| Timing | Cashnet net sales post **shortly after** the payment processes (fees the month after); the board gives no posting timeline for cash/check | **The month *after* the donation is received** |
| Rollover | ✅ Carries over between fiscal years | ✅ Carries over between fiscal years |
| Negative balance | 🚫 Never permitted | 🚫 Never permitted |

If the giver receives advertisement, logo placement, or event recognition, it is a **sponsorship** — that's
**fundraising, not a gift**, and it routes through the FTF, not Advancement.

> ⚠️ **Both pipelines still need Campus Life's blessing first if the money is external.** Sponsorships,
> donations, **and in-kind support** all require the Sponsorship Proposal Form before you pursue or accept
> anything — see **Sponsorships and external support** below.

## Fundraising

Two types:

1. **Product & event sales** — merchandise (t-shirts), event tickets, selling food, restaurant fundraiser
   nights.
2. **Sponsorships** — money or goods from a person or company in exchange for a **tangible benefit**
   (advertisement, logo placement, event recognition).

> **The Fundraising account "holds raised funds and no University-allocated funds."** That is the board's
> own framing, and it's the cleanest way to remember what belongs where: SU money lives in Operating,
> money you earned lives in Fundraising, donated money lives in Gift.

### 1. File an FTF fundraising request *before* you collect anything

**Submit a [Financial Transaction Form](ftf.md) before your group begins collecting any funds.** The point
is advance review: your Business Coordinator approves the collection process *before* it starts, not after.
The FTF is also the single form for card purchases, supplier payments, reallocations, and canceled-event
retrievals.

**What triggers it.** Any collection of money. The board's own worked examples, across the current cards
and quick-reference guides:

| Trigger | Where the board says so |
|---|---|
| Selling **merchandise** (t-shirts) | RULES: Fundraising (In-Depth) |
| Selling **event tickets** | RULES: Fundraising (In-Depth) |
| Selling **food** | RULES: Fundraising (In-Depth) |
| **Restaurant fundraiser nights** | RULES: Fundraising (In-Depth) |
| **Sponsorships** (benefit given in return) | RULES: Fundraising (In-Depth) |
| **Dues** | Deposit-timing checklists; Gift Account Quick Reference |
| **Merch sales** and **event fees** | Gift Account Quick Reference |
| **Co-sponsorship** money from another group or department | Gift Account Quick Reference |

The FTF's own purpose dropdown adds **"subsidize a purchase or event"**, **"selling items or services"**,
and **"other"** — field-by-field detail is in [ftf.md](ftf.md).

> ⚠️ **The live FTF is SSO-gated.** `forms.gle/gdFf8bBmXWKJ8nmd6` returns a Google sign-in wall to anyone
> not logged in with a WashU account, so an agent cannot read the current field list for you. Treat
> [ftf.md](ftf.md) as the record of those fields and re-confirm them yourself when you open the form.
> "Concessions" appears in [ftf.md](ftf.md) but **not anywhere on the live board** — if it matters to your
> collection, confirm with finance@su.wustl.edu.

**Which account do collected funds go into?** The FTF asks. The rule as recorded in [ftf.md](ftf.md) and
[accounts.md](accounts.md):

- **Operating** — if you **received** an SU allocation for the thing you're collecting toward.
- **Fundraising** — if you did **not**.

> ⚠️ **Conflict worth knowing about.** The board's Fundraising and Gift cards never state this
> Operating-vs-Fundraising rule; it comes from the FTF form itself. What the board *does* say points the
> same way — the Fundraising and Gift accounts hold "no University-allocated funds" — and the 80/20 revenue
> policy only makes sense if revenue from an SU-funded event is captured where SU can recoup it. Treat the
> rule as sound but confirm with your Business Coordinator on your first collection of the year.

### 2. Collect by approved method only

**Cash, Check, or Credit Card (Cashnet).** Those are **the only university-approved payment methods for
student organizations** — stated identically on the Operating, Fundraising, and Gift deposit cards.

> 🚫 **Prohibited: outside accounts and payment apps.** Student groups may use **only** the Operating,
> Fundraising, and Gift accounts in Workday. No personal checking accounts, no external bank accounts,
> no **Venmo**. The current cards name Venmo specifically; the fuller prior-year list also barred
> **PayPal, Cash App, GoFundMe, "or any similar platform"**, and prohibited opening any account in the
> University's name or under its **EIN/TIN**. If a group already has one, it must be **closed
> immediately** — ask your Business Coordinator to help transfer the funds.

> ⏳ **Bear Bucks is no longer on the approved-collection list.** Collecting via a Bear Bucks card reader
> (with its 5% Campus Card admin fee) appears **only on archived prior-year cards**. Every current card
> says Cash, Check, Cashnet. Separately, **spending** Meal Points / Bear Bucks is barred from all three
> accounts — see [restrictions.md](restrictions.md).

### 3. ⏰ Deposit inside 7 days — two branches, not one

| Collection type | Deposit within |
|---|---|
| **Tied to an event** | **7 days of the event taking place** |
| **Not tied to an event** (dues, merch sales) | **7 days of collecting the money** |

The quick-reference guides compress both into "within 7 days of collection or event completion." Sitting on
cash risks losing it and makes every balance you report wrong.

> 🚫 **Late deposits are a compliance issue, not a nuisance.** Deposit processing is one of the five
> privileges suspended during a compliance pause — see **Compliance** below.

### 4. Cash and check drop-off

1. **Where:** Danforth University Center (DUC) — reception desk on the **second floor of the Campus Life
   Suite, Room 270**.
2. **When:** **Monday–Friday, 8:30 a.m. – 5:00 p.m.**
3. **Who:** **Jordan**, the Administrative Coordinator, records the funds and **hands you a receipt for
   your records**. Keep it — it is your only proof until the deposit posts.
4. **Then:** your assigned **Business Coordinator** processes the deposit into the account you named on
   the FTF.

**Checks** must be payable to **Washington University**, with your **student group's name on the memo
line** — e.g. `Washington University – Troll Club`.

**Mailing address for checks** (give this to the issuer):

```
Washington University in St. Louis
1 Brookings Drive, MSC 1128-226-270
St. Louis, MO 63130
```

> ⚠️ **Do not confuse this with the gift mailing addresses.** Fundraising checks go to MSC 1128-226-270;
> **donations** go to Advancement at MSC 1082-414-2555 — a different address entirely, and using the wrong
> one misroutes the money and can cost the donor their tax credit.

### 5. Cashnet (collecting by credit card)

1. Submit the **FTF** and **upload the Cashnet Application** (the `.docx` is attached to the deposit cards
   on the board). The board's wording: *"you will need to upload the Cashnet Application"* — it is a
   **required attachment** whenever you are collecting by credit card.
2. Your **Business Coordinator reviews and approves**.
3. The team partners with **Accounting** to set up your link.
4. **Watch your inbox.** A representative from Accounting or the Business Operations team contacts you
   directly to finalize details and launch your **custom payment link**.

**What the Cashnet Application asks for:** student group name; your **project number and cost center**;
a named Cashnet contact (name, phone, email); event name, date, and description; **where you intend to
launch the link** (email / website / WUGO / social media / in person / other — and if in person, how);
the link's **launch and take-down dates**; whether attendance is capped and at what number; a description
of what is being marketed; and the **selling amount**. Three signatures close it out: the group's Cashnet
contact, the **group treasurer**, and the **SU Business Coordinator**.

Two things to plan for:

- **Cashnet is for purchases and sales — never donations.** Route monetary gifts to the Gift Department at
  **gifts.wustl.edu** so the donor receives proper tax credit.
- **A credit card processing fee comes off the top.** **Net sales post to your account shortly after the
  payment processes; the fees appear the following month.** Price for the fee up front — the Cashnet
  Application explicitly tells you to build **sales tax and the Cashnet fee into the price**.

> ⚠️ **The board contradicts itself on the fee rate.** The current PROCESS cards and the Fundraising
> Processes Quick Reference say **"about 3%."** The **Cashnet Application form itself** — the current
> attachment you are required to submit — says **"CashNet 2% fee,"** as did the archived prior-year cards.
> Board version kept above; **confirm the live rate with finance@su.wustl.edu or your Business Coordinator
> before you set a ticket price**, because the gap decides whether you clear your target.

### 6. The revenue split — plan for it before you sell a ticket

Straight from the **Financial Policies** card (FY27):

- For **any SU-funded event**, **80% of any revenue generated returns to Student Union**; **20% must go
  into the student group's fundraising account**.
- If the 80% SU would collect **exceeds its principal allocation**, the **overage** goes to your
  **fundraising account** — SU only ever recoups what it actually allocated.
- **Revenue** includes but is not limited to: funds from **ticket sales or similar processes**, **monetary
  awards** (to the group as a whole *or* to individual participants), and **any other funds generated by
  hosting or participating in** an SU-funded event.

> 💡 **The escape hatch is real and it is written down: "If a student group utilizes their fundraising or
> gift accounts to host fundraisers, they will keep 100% of the revenue generated."** Fund the event out
> of Fundraising or Gift instead of an SU allocation and the 80/20 split does not apply at all.

Practical consequence: a ticketed, **SU-funded** event mostly earns money for SU. If building your
fundraising balance is the goal, run it on your own money — that flips you from 20% to 100%. Weigh that
against the fact that **Operating cannot pay for fundraising efforts anyway** — see
[restrictions.md](restrictions.md).

## Sponsorships and external support — approval comes first

> Groups seeking **external sponsorships, donations, or in-kind support** must contact **Scott Williams in
> Campus Life** — Assistant Director for Student Groups and Student Involvement,
> scott.williams@wustl.edu — and complete a **Sponsorship Proposal Form** **before pursuing or accepting**
> support.
>
> **Sponsorship approvals are limited, rare, and require advance university approval.** Groups should
> **first explore Student Union funding and other internal fundraising options.**

This appears on the Fundraising rules card, the Gift rules card, the Gift deposit card, and both quick
reference guides — it is the most repeated sentence in this corner of the board, which tells you how often
it gets broken.

Three ways groups break it by accident:

- Emailing a company to ask before the form is in.
- Accepting something already offered ("they came to *us*") without checking.
- Treating **in-kind** support — donated food, donated printing, a free venue — as not a sponsorship. It
  is covered.

> ⚠️ **The board does not publish the Sponsorship Proposal Form's location or its fields.** Get both from
> Scott Williams or your Campus Life Advisor; don't guess at them.

## How donors give a gift

All donations process through **University Advancement** so donors receive their **official tax credits**.
You do not collect, hold, or forward gift money — you point donors at a channel and wait.

**Online:** the WashU giving form. The donor checks **"I prefer to enter my own designation (specify
below)"** and types **your exact student group name** into the open field so funds route to your
organization.

**By phone:** toll-free **(877) 215-2727**, choose **option 3**. Visa, MasterCard, Discover, and American
Express accepted. The donor provides their own card number, expiration date, and name as it appears on the
card — **you should never collect or relay a donor's card details on their behalf.**

**By mail:** the donor prints and completes the **printable gift form (PDF)** and includes a credit card
payment or a check **payable to Washington University**.

| Carrier | Address |
|---|---|
| **FedEx / UPS** | MSC 1082-414-2555, Washington University, 7425 Forsyth Blvd., Clayton, MO 63105 |
| **USPS** | MSC 1082-414-2555, Washington University, 1 Brookings Drive, St. Louis, MO 63130-9989 |

### ⏳ Gift money is slow — budget for the lag

> **"Because charitable gifts go through a thorough verification process with University Advancement and
> the central Gift Department, they take a little extra time to route. Typically, you can expect to see the
> funds officially reflect in your student group's Gift account during the month after the donation is
> received."**

So a gift secured in October is generally not spendable until November. **Do not commit against a promised
donation** — approval and available funds must both precede spending
([su-finance-overview.md](su-finance-overview.md)).

> ⚠️ **Board authoring slip to ignore, not follow.** The **Gift** Account Quick Reference tells you to
> "Deposit all collected money (dues, merch, event fees, co-sponsorships) into your **Gift** Account within
> 7 days" — but dues, merch, and event fees are *fundraising*, and gift money never passes through your
> hands to deposit. This reads as text copied from the Fundraising guide. The 7-day rule is real; its
> application to a Gift account is not. Confirm with finance@su.wustl.edu if you have a genuine
> hand-collected co-sponsorship payment and are unsure which account it belongs in.

## Spending from Fundraising and Gift accounts

Same three methods as Operating — **WashU credit card** (Thursdays & Fridays, 10:00 a.m.–12:00 p.m. and
2:00 p.m.–4:00 p.m., DUC 270, FTF at least **one day** ahead, no same-day purchases), **Workday expense
report reimbursement** (within **7 days** of purchase; requires being an active **student employee** to
log in), and **supplier payment** by ACH or check (invoice + W-9 + ACH form on an FTF within **7 days**).
Full detail in [spending-methods.md](spending-methods.md).

There is **no separate rulebook for these accounts.** The Fundraising and Gift spending cards are
word-for-word identical to the Operating one — receipts, rental cars, Airbnb, travel booking accounts,
the 25-line/one-event expense report limit, the one-per-year late-submission "lifeline." Everything in
[spending-methods.md](spending-methods.md) applies unchanged.

### 🟢 Neither account may go below $0

**The Fundraising account is not permitted to carry a negative balance. Neither is the Gift account.**
Same rule as Operating. Combined with "approval before spending," you cannot front a purchase against
money you expect to raise or expect a donor to send.

### 🚫 Raising the money yourself unlocks less than groups think

The **Restrictions on Accounts** list has two tiers, and only one of them is about Operating:

- **Barred from Operating only** — including **"Fundraising efforts"** and **"Donations."** So the *cost of
  running a fundraiser* has to come out of Fundraising or Gift; SU money cannot pay for it. That is a large
  part of what these accounts are for.
  - **Careful with donations, though.** "Donations" is barred from Operating, but **"donation payment
    reimbursements" are barred from all three accounts** — so you cannot pay a charity out of pocket and
    get reimbursed from Fundraising or Gift either. Ask your Business Coordinator how to route a genuine
    charitable payout before you promise one.
- **Barred from Operating, Fundraising, AND Gift** — alcohol and drinkware, dangerous/harmful items,
  donation payment reimbursements, gifts/gift cards/prizes **on the WashU credit card**, purchases using
  Meal Points/Bear Bucks, security deposits, service-provider reimbursements, and **payments to WashU
  employees including student employees**.

Full lists in [restrictions.md](restrictions.md). The Fundraising card and the Gift card each carry their
own copy of "Check Before You Check Out!", worded for their own account — SU is explicit that the
restriction list binds Fundraising and Gift money too.

> 💡 **Where fundraising money *is* a legitimate top-up — and where it isn't.** The Financial Policies card
> says the caps are limits on **what SU will allocate**, not on what an event may cost: *"Student groups
> can feel free to supplement SU's funding with their own fundraising, Flat Funding, or other externally
> gifted funds beyond what SU provides."* So your own money can carry an event past an SU cap. It
> **cannot** buy a restricted item, and it cannot be spent during a **spending blackout** — the blackout
> dates on the board apply to "your Operating, **Fundraising, or Gift** accounts" alike. See
> [calendar.md](calendar.md) for this year's blackout windows.

> ⚠️ **Archived-only rule, flagged because it is expensive if true.** Prior-year cards stated that events
> must be registered and approved in **WUGO** before collecting money *or* making event-related purchases,
> and that for a **non-sanctioned** event "the group must cover the expenses using their Fundraising and/or
> Gift accounts. If these funds are insufficient, members will need to use personal funds." No **current**
> Fundraising or Gift card repeats either sentence. WUGO registration is still a live requirement on its
> own track ([campus-life.md](campus-life.md)) — but whether Fundraising/Gift is still the designated
> fallback for an unsanctioned event needs confirming with finance@su.wustl.edu.

### You have to drive the transaction yourself

All financial transactions from your **Operating, Fundraising, or Gift** accounts must be **initiated and
managed by your student leaders**. Advisors, academic departments, and campus partners **cannot** make
purchases on your behalf or process transactions out of your accounts — even when they are the ones
advising the project, and even when the money in question is money you raised. They must still follow every
standard SU rule, timeline, and procedure. See [treasurer-role.md](treasurer-role.md).

## Compliance — what a pause costs you

Falling out of compliance **temporarily suspends five things**, and three of them are how money reaches
these accounts at all:

- 💳 WashU credit card usage
- 💸 Reimbursement processing
- 🤝 Supplier payments
- 📥 **Deposit processing** — you cannot add funds
- 🔄 Reallocations

Privileges resume as soon as the issue is fixed. **Misuse of funds, fraudulent activity, or intentional
mismanagement** is a different matter — it is referred immediately to **Campus Life and University
Administration**. Unresolved routine issues are also referred to Campus Life for review.

## If the group disbands

Leftover **Fundraising** and **Gift** money stays on campus: it transfers to a general **Division of
Student Success** account, and the **Vice Chancellor for Student Success** reallocates it to other student
programs, campus events, and active clubs.

> ⚠️ **Corrected here, and stale elsewhere.** Both account-balance cards say **"Division of Student
> Success."** [accounts.md](accounts.md) still says "Office of Student Success" — the board's wording is
> the one to trust. Note also that the **Gift** Account Balances card describes leftover money in "their
> **Fundraising** account," and its balance section says "**Operating** account balances are updated every
> weekday" — both are copy-paste artifacts on the board, not a statement that Gift balances behave
> differently. All three account reports refresh **every weekday, Monday–Friday**.
